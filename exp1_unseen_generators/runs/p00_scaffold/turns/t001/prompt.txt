TASK. You are given the weights of an audio deepfake detector, {checkpoint_name}. It maps a waveform to one scalar score. A higher score means the clip is more likely to be synthetic speech. Your job is to make that score more general by EDITING THE WEIGHTS DIRECTLY: find the directions in the network that the score uses as shortcuts, and remove those shortcuts without giving up the ability to tell synthetic speech from real speech.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, the edited weights and the original weights are scored on held-out clips you never see. The headline number is the equal error rate (EER) on generators that were not in the practice set. EER is a ranking measure: adding a constant to every score, or applying any strictly increasing function of the score, does not change it. Around that number, a shortcut report is computed on the same clips:

- speaker-disjoint matched EER: generators that were in the practice set, speakers that were not
- unseen-generator EER: generators that were not in the practice set
- gender probe: on real clips only, how well the score ranks one gender above the other (AUC; 0.5 means the score carries no gender ranking)
- language probe: the same, for language, on real clips only
- speaker dependence: on real clips only, the share of score variance explained by speaker identity (0 means none, 1 means the score is constant on each speaker)
- same-transcript EER: EER using only clips that share a transcript with the other class, so the words do not decide the label
- cross-transcript correlation: across transcripts, the correlation between the mean score of the real clips and the mean score of the synthetic clips. A high correlation means the score is tracking the words

An edit counts only if the unseen-generator EER goes down, the matched EER does not get materially worse, and none of the shortcut measures get materially worse. A lower EER that comes from leaning harder on speaker, gender, language, or the transcript does not count.

WHAT COUNTS. We want edits that come from a measured shortcut. Show that some direction, channel, head, or block predicts speaker, gender, language, or transcript on the practice clips, and that changing it moves that shortcut more than it moves the matched detection score. Out of scope, and refused by save_edit: every tensor listed as frozen, and any edit that only multiplies a whole tensor by a constant.
